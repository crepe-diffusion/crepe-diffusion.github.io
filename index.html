CREPE: Controlling Diffusion with Replica Exchange

Overview

Inference-time control of diffusion models aims to steer model outputs to satisfy new constraints without retraining. CREPE is a flexible approach based on replica exchange (parallel tempering): it enables principled control of diffusion models, maintains high diversity of generated samples, and supports online refinement or early termination.

What you can do with CREPE

Reward tilting / prompt alignment

Steer the sample towards a desired reward or prompt.

Inverse problem solving

Solve inverse problems with the diffusion model prior.

Temperature annealing (powering)

Sharpen distributions to a lower temperature level.

Model composition

Compose objectives via product of diffusion experts.

CFG debiasing

Debias the classifier-free guidance (CFG) to achieve better conditioning on the class labels.

Modality-agnostic control

Control models for both continuous and discrete diffusion models within a unified framework.

Key idea: exchange across diffusion times

CREPE runs multiple chains at different diffusion steps (noise levels). Each chain performs local updates, and we periodically propose swaps between neighbouring noise levels with an acceptance rule. This enables principled communication between easy-to-mix (high-noise) and high-fidelity (low-noise) states.

Experiments

We evaluate CREPE across multiple modalities and control objectives: (i) inference-time tempering for Boltzmann sampling of molecules, (ii) debiasing classifier-free guidance (CFG) for image generation, (iii) prompted reward-tilting for fine-grained alignment, (iv) reward-based model composition for long-horizon maze trajectory stitching with online refinement, and (v) discrete diffusion control (CTMC / mask diffusion).

Inference-time tempering for Boltzmann sampling (molecules)

We temper pretrained diffusion models to sample from lower-temperature Boltzmann distributions for alanine dipeptide, tetrapeptide, and hexapeptide. We report discrepancies between generated samples and MD baselines using energy/distance histogram distances (e.g., TVD), sample distances (e.g., W2), and 2D TICA-based distribution metrics (e.g., MMD).

  • Task: high-temp model → low-temp target via tempering.
  • Metrics: energy TVD, distance TVD, sample W2, TICA-MMD.
  • Takeaway: CREPE is competitive with SMC baselines and maintains strong coverage / diversity after burn-in.

Debiasing classifier-free guidance (CFG) for ImageNet

We apply CREPE to debias CFG and evaluate ImageReward (IR), CLIP score, and FID on ImageNet-64 and ImageNet-512. CREPE typically needs a burn-in period, but scales well with more samples and maintains higher within-batch diversity.

Prompted reward-tilting for image generation

We combine CFG debiasing with prompted reward-tilting using ImageReward to steer generations toward more detailed instructions. We track reward alignment (IR) and semantic alignment (CLIP) along PT iterations, observing improved prompt satisfaction after burn-in while maintaining diversity.

  • Models: ImageNet-64 (pixel-space) and ImageNet-512 (latent diffusion).
  • Signals: IR / CLIP vs. PT iterations; qualitative trajectories across iterations.

Model composition for maze navigation (trajectory stitching)

We stitch short trajectory segments in a large 2D maze using an unconditional diffusion model and a reward-based composition objective. CREPE enables coherent long-horizon planning via swap-based communication across diffusion times, and supports online refinement by adding new constraints during sampling.

  • Dataset: pointmaze-giant-stitch-v0.
  • Objective: reward-based constraints on start/goal and segment connectivity.
  • Online refinement: add an intermediate waypoint reward mid-run and adapt quickly.

CREPE on discrete diffusion (CTMC / mask diffusion on MNIST)

We extend CREPE to discrete diffusion modeled as a continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC), and apply it to debias CFG for mask diffusion on MNIST. Qualitatively, CREPE produces plausible samples and shows slightly higher diversity compared to SMC baselines under comparable compute budgets.
